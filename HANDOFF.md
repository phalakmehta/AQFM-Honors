## 3. What has been done

**Where we stand (discussed 2026-11-05):** model changes on Delhi have reached a plateau.
- **Model size barely matters:** v1 has 20× v2's learned numbers, yet v2 is only 5.8% worse at 24 h. v2-M is within 0.7–1.8% of v1. Its 24 h gap (+0.22) is smaller than the single-seed spread (std about 0.8).
- **Every model misses the same days:** Very Poor + Severe days are 34% of forecasts but 61–64% of the total 24 h error. This holds for v1, v2, v2-M, XGBoost and PatchTST alike.
- **Information matters more:** future weather cuts v2-M's 24 h error by 15% (38.09 → 32.26).
- **Conclusion:** the limit is the information in the inputs, not model capacity. More size or step tuning on Delhi is expected to stay within seed noise.
- **For the thesis:** v2-M matches v1 at about 1/5 of v1's size, and beats v1 without weather (by up to 3.8% at 72 h).

## 7. Things learned the hard way

- `train.py` appends to `log.jsonl`. After a crash or power cut, move the interrupted run folder aside before rerunning into the same path.
- Every run and result records `code` (git commit + dirty flag) at its start. Commit code before launching a chain, or the results cannot be registered.
- Validation every 1,000 steps; the cosine learning-rate schedule spans `--steps`. A longer schedule is not "the same run, continued".
- Single seeds differ by about 0.8 µg/m³ at 24 h. Never compare models on one seed when the gap is that small.
- Check `git status` before telling the user something is committed. Check `git ls-remote origin` before saying it is pushed.
